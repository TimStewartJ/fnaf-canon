# Reader's memo: the Unified Model, read from the first page to the last

Read on 3 October as plain text and in the site's order: the intro, the part blurbs, the twenty beats, then the
thirty-five chapters. The planning papers were opened only afterwards. The conclusions are not questioned here. Every
note is about how the text reads.

## How to use this memo

- Words quoted from the theory are in «guillemets», exactly as they print. A rewrite follows the word **Fix**. A
  short one is given in the line, inside double quotation marks. A long one is set in a quoted block.
- In a rewrite, ¹ stands for a citation that is already on that clause. Keep the token you have. No rewrite adds a
  fact, a record or a quotation. Where a rewrite names a game or glosses a term, the words come from the chapter that
  owns the thing: check them against your record before you paste.
- Inside a rewrite, words in their own double quotation marks are the evidence's, unchanged.
- Links and entity tokens are written as you will find them. In section 3 that is `theory.ts`: ((id)) and {{id}}. In
  section 4 it is the printed chapter: →id and {id}.
- Each chapter is tagged with its writer. W1: the frame (`story.ts`, `apparatus.ts`, and here also the intro, the
  blurbs and the storyline in `theory.ts`). W2: makers and underground. W3: family and machines. W4: murders and
  night shift. W5: the reckoning. W6: the afterimage.
- Every quotation matches the text as it printed at 06:35 on 3 October. The chapters have been changing since about
  07:15, some of them as these notes were applied. If a quoted sentence has changed, apply the note to the sentence
  that replaced it, and skip a note whose change is already made.
- Two readers are meant throughout. **The fan** played the first three or four games years ago and reads on a phone.
  **The obsessive** knows every theory and reads to find the weak point.
- Notes inside a chapter are ranked: the first gives the reader most.

## 1. The verdict

1. **Compelling: yes.** From the second beat the storyline pulls, and most story chapters open on a scene that makes
   the reader want the rest.
2. **Readable: mostly.** The sentences are short and plain. What slows the reader is structure: a chapter often says
   the same thing in its body, in its compare box and again under "what pushes back".
3. **Enjoyable: yes** where it tells a scene and where its dry humour shows (the small print of the job
   advertisement, "There will be cake!", "Eggs Benedict"). Less so where it audits itself.
4. **Best, one: the scene-first chapters.** `michael`, `bite`, `child-outside`, `two-machines` and the opening of
   `mimic` are as good as this kind of writing gets.
5. **Best, two: honesty used as argument.** The text names its own weakest points before the obsessive can: «it is
   the weakest point of the model's reading» (`ucn`), «the model has no account of it» (`vanny`), «no act»
   (`november-1987`). I could not find a dodge that the text had not found first.
6. **Best, three: the spine.** Beat 2 and beat 20 answer each other, and `patterns` holds the sentence that ties the
   whole together: «The makers in this story are fathers, and each of them loses a child.»
7. **In the way, one: the triple telling.** Twenty of the thirty-five "what pushes back" paragraphs run past 150
   words (the mean is about 165, and `ucn` has 312). Fifteen chapters use theirs to say again what the body or the
   compare box has said.
8. **In the way, two: the front door.** Four paragraphs of how-to-read; a first beat about a firm the fan has never
   heard of, in a game the beat does not name; and a first chapter that tells the story a third time, grades it on a
   scale the reader was not given, and carries a note about what an earlier draft said.
9. **In the way, three: the archive's filing voice inside the story.** "The model" or "the model's" appears 267
   times, more than fifty of them only to say what the model will not do. Over fifty asides say how a record is held
   («on record here as a transcript» and its kin, twenty times in twelve chapters). One hidden tape of the Cassette
   Man's is introduced as if new in nine chapters.
10. **None of this needs a different book.** The conclusions, the order and nearly every sentence can stay. The work
    is to cut second and third tellings, to give each quotation one home, and to let the first page point at five
    things that now sit in the last part.

## 2. Across the whole text

### 2.1 Eight decisions for every writer

**A. "What pushes back" names the records and stops.**
The page prints the counter records and then your paragraph. Where the body has a compare box, the paragraph must
not weigh the rival a second time. Give each counter record a clause. Give the model's answer only where the body
has not. Say what is left unanswered, and stop. The style guide asked for two to five sentences. A chapter with nine
counter records needs more than five, and none needs 250 words: the rewrites in section 4 take three of the
longest from 312 words to 216, from 247 to 203 and from 218 to 140. Fifteen chapters replay their own body and need
this most: `killer`, `plush`, `nightmares`, `bite`, `child-outside`, `golden`, `november-1987`, `springtrap`,
`fazbears-fright`, `ffps`, `salvage`, `ucn`, `digital`, `vanny`, `canon-rules`. Each has a note in section 4, most
with the paragraph written out.

- Before (`nightmares`): the compare box says «The attackers are called Nightmare.» and «Flowers, a bottle of pills
  or a drip stand can appear beside the bed». The paragraph under it says «The attackers' own name is Nightmare» and
  «Flowers, pills and a drip stand can appear by the bed».
- After: see `nightmares`, note 1.

**B. One home for each quotation, scene and caveat.**
Table 2.2 gives the home. Everywhere else, a clause and a link. `plush` shows how it is done: «→afton-house tells
that room in full. What matters here is in the plush's lap.»

**C. The model takes positions. It does not narrate its own silence.**
Keep "the model reads", "the model leans", and "the model does not choose" where a reader expects a choice. Cut the
tail that only adds that the model adds nothing.

- Before (`springtrap`): «What "the only thing that they had" was, the tape does not say, and the model adds
  nothing.»
- After: "The tape does not say what "the only thing that they had" was."
- Before (`november-1987`): «It does not say by whom or for what, and the model adds nothing.»
- After: "It does not say by whom or for what."

One abstention a chapter is plenty, and the strongest may stay. `michael`'s «Why he does not die, no game says, and
the model does not guess» earns its place.

**D. Say how a record is held once, then use a short tag.**
The asides are honest and they stay. They are now said in a dozen forms: «on record here as a transcript», «kept here
as a transcript», «read here from images of the documents», «not yet seen in play», «placement unconfirmed», «was not
checked», «a label not yet checked in play», «as described», «with no capture on file here», «counted on one
screenshot», «checked here on one screenshot», «by the wiki's account». W1 defines four tags once, in how-to-read and
in `canon-rules`. Every chapter then uses the tag, in brackets, after the fact it qualifies.

| Tag | Means |
|---|---|
| (a transcript) | Scott's 2018 and 2024 interviews, held as machine transcripts of archived video |
| (read from an image) | A document or texture not yet seen in play |
| (as described) | A scene the archive holds as a description and has not captured |
| (a fan wiki's account) | A count or a comparison the archive has taken from a wiki |

- Before (`fazbears-fright`): «In a 2018 interview, on record here as a transcript, Scott said of FNaF3»
- After: "In a 2018 interview (a transcript) Scott said of FNaF3"
- Before (`pizzaplex`): "As described, the body is marked "PROTOTYPE" ¹, «a label not yet checked in play»."
- After: "The body is marked "PROTOTYPE" ¹ (as described)."

`fazbears-fright` already writes «(a transcript)» once, so the tag is not new to the text.

**E. Mark an inference once.**
The three markers work when each appears once, where the inference enters. They blur in three ways.

1. Pile-up. `murders` has "most likely" eleven times, twice in one sentence and at the head of two headings in a row.
2. Two markers on one claim. The answer of `afton-house` opens «Most likely, yes» and closes «is the model's reading
   of that». `springlocks` says «It is most likely Murray's firm» in the body and «is the model's reading» under
   "what pushes back".
3. Words the first page never defined: "lean" and "leans" (21 times), "loosely" (19), "narrowly", "at a low grade",
   "preference".

Decision: one marker for one inference, in the sentence where it enters. Two inferences keep their two markers, in
two sentences. W1 adds "leans" to how-to-read (see 3.1), since the text cannot do without it.

**F. Lead the answer with the verdict.**
A reader should be able to repeat the answer to a friend. `michael` does it in 31 words. `partners` takes 98 and
gives its verdicts last. `remnant` puts «No game says what Remnant is» third. `nightmares` answers a yes-or-no
question in its second sentence. Rule: the verdict first, the grounds second, the limits last, and about 60 words.
Fifteen answers pass 70. Rewrites are under the chapters.

**G. Turn the sentence round only once a chapter.**
"What X is, no game says" is plain and honest, and it is everywhere: over thirty times, six of them in the twenty
beats, twice in `faith` and twice in `ucn`. Keep it for the one silence that matters most in a chapter. Elsewhere
write it forwards.

- Before (beat 10): «What was done that week, no record says.»
- After: "No record says what was done that week."

**H. Furniture.**

- A caption says what to look at. It does not report the archive's checking («Where in the building it hangs was not
  checked») and it does not repeat the paragraph beneath it (`killer`).
- A table cell is a sentence or less. Three tables break this and are hard on a phone: `patterns` (cells of 54 to 88
  words), `games` (four columns, eighteen rows) and `pizzaplex` (cells of up to 34 words).
- A heading says what the section finds. Most do, and the best are findings in five words: «Purple is a way of
  drawing», «Awake, by a short lead», «The same trick twice», «Sealed is not cut off». Those that only name a topic
  are listed under their chapters.
- A sentence does not begin with an entity token that prints in lower case (`ffps`, `child-outside`: see section 5).
