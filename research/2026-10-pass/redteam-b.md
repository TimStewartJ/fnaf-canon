## R12. Order of events

**Verdict: holds with changes.** FNaF2, then FNaF1, then FNaF3 stands on six independent joins. Nothing in the
games pulls against it; Scott's store text for FNaF2 reads the other way and should be listed (point 6).
Elizabeth before Michael's week, the speech after the Fazbear's Fright fire, and
the killer dead before the attraction opens are fixed. The two conventions are labelled as conventions.

1. **Two of the arguments for dating the killer's death do not work.** R12 says "after his death the ghosts
   'fade away'" and that the rival placement "has FNaF1's four rebuilt and haunted again after the ghosts have
   gone" (`fnaf3-n5-springlock-death`). R14 and R30 have those children still bound thirty years later and in
   Pizzeria Simulator, so on the model's own account the ghosts had not gone. The other argument is that the
   four are "whole and in their FNaF1 places" (`fnaf3-eon-map-fnaf1-layout`). R12 also rules that FNaF1's
   building is the old location, where the same four stood whole before they were moved to the 1987 restaurant
   (`fnaf2-call2-older-models`), so that picture fits the rot before 1987 equally well. What is left is R9's
   living culprit in November 1987 (medium), against a rival with `scott-fnaf2-trailer-captions`
   ("dismantled"), the faceless older models of 1987 (`fnaf2-parts-service-old-models`) and a building "left
   to rot" (`fnaf2-call1-new-location`). *Fix:* drop both arguments, say the placement rests on R9, and rate
   it "medium, lower end".

2. **The fourth clipping is dated by assumption, and half of section 3 hangs on it.** FNaF1 shows four
   clippings the same way and dates none of them. R8 and step 10 read two as the old restaurant's history
   before 1987 (`fnaf1-news-uphill-battle`, `fnaf1-news-sanitation`). R12, R24(3), rows 22, 24, 25 and 28 and
   step 14 read the fourth, `fnaf1-news-closing`, as news from FNaF1's own year; the "Year of FNaF1" label in
   its `when` field is the record writer's reading. Read as old news like its neighbours, it describes step 10
   almost word for word (a long struggle, a year with no buyer, then a building left to rot), it leaves
   FNaF1's own closing with no record, and its "many years ago" would put the five well before the
   mid-1980s. The split may be right: FNaF1's rationed power (`scott-fnaf1-steam-budget-cuts`) and Scott's
   "thirty years after Freddy Fazbear's Pizza closed" (`scott-fnaf3-steam-description`) favour it. *Fix:* argue
   it in R12, list the other reading as the alternative, and mark step 14 "I", not "F".

3. **Circus Baby's slot: records are missing on both sides.**
   - For the model's order: HandUnit's "another contender in children's entertainment" most naturally names
     the pizzeria venture as well as the rentals (`sl-handunit-freddy-fazbear-closing`). And the Cassette Man
     states an order that no ruling cites: "As if what he had already done wasn't enough, he found a new way
     to desecrate" (`ffps-insanity-new-way-to-desecrate`, absent from `model.md`). It orders the use of the
     souls after the murders; it does not date the building of the machines.
   - Against it: step 7 has the boy "watched from his father's company" in c. 1983, from a room in the
     building that houses the business HandUnit dates after a Freddy's closing (`sl-private-room-keypad-1983`,
     `sl-extras-map-entertainment-and-rental`). R3's own table lists "that anyone watched in 1983" as
     inferred. And `sl-springlock-suit-from-old-pizzeria` is filed as support ("already thought 'too
     dangerous' to use"), but "too dangerous" is Baby's present opinion. What the record shows is that a
     springlock suit was supplied to her pizzeria, which fits the springlock years before row 15's retirement
     at least as well.
   - *Fix:* cite the two supporting records; say what the model assumes about the facility in 1983 (the
     building is older than the rental business, or the watching is later than 1983), or soften step 7 to R3's
     wording; take the springlock suit out of the support list and say it cuts both ways.

4. **"A place after step 14 is equally allowed" is not true once R3 is counted.** R3 counts the pink bedroom
   as the daughter's (`fnaf4-second-bedroom`). If she is alive and small c. 1983, she cannot be the little
   girl of Baby's one day after FNaF1's restaurant has closed, except on the tightest compressed reading,
   which R24 leans against. If she is already dead in 1983, the venture is older than any Freddy's closing the
   model has. *Fix:* "a later place is allowed only if the pink room is not hers."

5. **One row holds two orderings with different support.** The row "Pizzeria Simulator after that speech; UCN
   after Pizzeria Simulator" is rated medium-high on "Inference (R13, R16)". The first needs the volunteer to
   be Michael (R13, medium). The second stands whoever the player is (`ucn-roster-includes-ffps-four`,
   `ucn-orville-he-tried-to-release`, `scott-2018-ucn-for-pizzeria-simulator`). *Fix:* split them: medium and
   medium-high. Pizzeria Simulator after the Fazbear's Fright fire is firmer than "after that speech"; row 32
   and step 20 should say so.

6. **Small.** "Seven joins" lists six (the memo's seventh was the wage, colour only). "Inference from three
   records" lists four, one of which, the FNaF2 cutscenes, R25 rates low-medium. "Records against" has no
   entry for the first ordering. There is one: Scott's FNaF2 store text is written as a sequel's ("the old and
   aging animatronics are joined by a new cast", `scott-fnaf2-steam-old-and-aging`). The cheque overrides it,
   and the ruling should say so.

3. **The row "UCN's player is Michael" reads "No (R16)".** That enters a medium ruling as a flat No, and the
   brief has "Not UCN's
   player". *Fix:* "probably not (R16)".

## R14. FNaF3's good ending against Pizzeria Simulator

**Verdict: holds with changes.** An intended release that the later games do not treat as final is the right
shape, and Scott's "complex" is used fairly.

1. **The storyline states the good ending as an event.** Step 17 is marked F: "In the attraction's last week
   the children are given cake and six of them leave". The brief has "Happiest Day; the fire. Firm." R14
   rates the ending as intended (medium-high); R25 lists "which FNaF3 ending happened" as left open by Scott
   on purpose (`scott-2018-interview-fnaf3-ending-unanswered`); the game's default screen is captioned "Bad
   ending." (`fnaf3-bad-ending-heads`); and `fnaf3-happiest-day` is recorded as an undated past scene. *Fix:*
   F for the attraction, the find and the fire; the cake and Happiest Day take R14's marks.

2. **R14 rejects as "the telling" the account that R15 and R30 give.** R14's surviving alternative is that
   "what Pizzeria Simulator gathers is something else: what the killer 'robbed' from them". R30 rules that
   Molten Freddy holds "what was taken from the five", and R15 that Remnant is something "drawn off and put
   somewhere else". R14 also says "Why the first did not hold is not stated", while the Cassette Man gives a
   reason in outline: "they will never find rest now. Not like this" (`ffps-insanity-tiny-breaths`), after "he
   robbed them of the only thing that they had. Again." (`ffps-insanity-trap-again`). *Fix:* choose. Either
   R14 adopts that account at low ("no rest while what he took from them is in use"), or R30 and R15 stop
   saying "what was taken".

3. **The present-tense argument does not separate the readings.** "Now" and "He lured them all back"
   (`ffps-insanity-lured-back`) fit a freeing followed by a luring back as well as they fit no freeing at all.

4. **"Gives the children their graves only after its own fire."** The stones are shown after the credits
   (`ffps-lorekeeper-gravestones`). No record says when they were set, and children missing for decades could
   have had stones for decades. *Fix:* "shows the graves only after its fire". The thesis sentence is a figure
   of speech and should not become an event in a chapter.

## R15. What Remnant is

**Verdict: holds with changes.** The core is right: a substance, a machine named for a purpose, a hedged risk
note, and no statement that it holds the dead. The wording goes past the sheet in four places and the reading
imports two things it should not.

1. **"The games say three things and no more."** The sheet (read from the image) has four labelled parts and
   six sentences of notes. The ruling leaves out "When heated, no observable motion", "Over-usage/
   Over-exposure negates effect" and "malleable, but not more" (`ffps-scup-remnant-reservoir-notes`). The first
   is the most suggestive line on the page. *Fix:* list what the sheet says; drop "three" and "no more".

2. **Design notes are told as practice.** "A physical substance that Afton Robotics kept heated in a tank and
   injected in trace amounts with the Scooper." The sheet prescribes ("Keep in heated tank", "Leave trace line
   amount on interior"). No game shows an injection, and Sister Location shows the machine only emptying what
   is put in front of it (`sl-ballora-scooped`, `sl-real-ending-scooped-mirror`). *Fix:* "a blueprint
   specifies".

3. **"The machine is Sister Location's scooper (`sl-scooping-room-explained`)."** The cited record does not say
   so, and the sheet never prints the word; the link is the acronym, the "Excavating Arm" and the Afton
   Robotics mark. *Fix:* mark it as a strong inference.

4. **"What the dead leave in the metal."** No record mentions metal, or any source for the substance. That
   phrase is the novels' idea of Remnant. *Fix:* "in the machines", or drop the clause. The brief repeats it.

5. **"Nobody designed the first hauntings"** (thesis: "Nobody designed that", high). The record is one man's
   ignorance (`ffps-insanity-tiny-breaths`), and he is the builder, not the killer. The model's own step 8 has
   an agent: the Puppet, in a minigame called "Give Gifts, Give Life" (`fnaf2-give-gifts-give-life`), and "the
   way you lifted others into yours" (`ffps-completion-shut-out`). No record speaks to the killer's intent
   either way. *Fix:* "The man who built the machines says he does not know how it happened; what the games
   show is the Puppet giving the dead children their masks." Rate the claim that the killer did not design it
   as an argument from silence, low-medium.

6. **"William Afton then tried to manufacture the effect".** "Then" is R12's low convention inside a
   low-medium reading. *Fix:* cite `ffps-insanity-new-way-to-desecrate`, the one record that states an order,
   and say what it covers (R12, point 3). R5 pulls the other way: it reads "I will put you back together"
   (`fnaf4-i-will-put-you-back-together`) as the father's phrase, said c. 1983 at step 7, which the model's
   order puts before the five. (Taken from report A, R5 problem 6, and checked here.)

7. **One record, two readings** (`ffps-scrap-baby-completion-monologue`, `ffps-scrap-baby-make-you-proud-daddy`).
   R15: Baby "treats 'all of those little souls' as something to collect in order to 'be complete'". R30: the
   little souls "in one place" are the five. R32: "Now we can do what we were created to do" shows the
   Funtimes were built to take children. The Cassette Man's answer is about children she expects to find:
   "chasing the cries of children in some unseen chamber... you will never find them"
   (`ffps-completion-chasing-cries`), after Tutorial Unit's "big party here Saturday"
   (`ffps-tutorial-saturday-party`). On that reading the little souls are the party's children, which is R32's
   sense and not R15's or R30's. *Fix:* pick one reading for all three rulings.

## R16. Ultimate Custom Night

**Verdict: confidence should change.** William is still the better reading of the office. "Medium" does not
survive, for a reason of logic and because the table of lines is scored more one-sidedly than the analyst's
memo it came from.

**The case for Michael, as its advocate would put it.** Pizzeria Simulator's player is Michael (R13) and
chooses to stay in the fire. UCN was built as that game's custom night, and its store text says "you will once
again be trapped alone in an office" (`scott-2018-ucn-for-pizzeria-simulator`,
`scott-ucn-store-once-again-trapped`). The man in the office is told what Michael was told and nobody else:
"You won't die" (`ucn-scrap-baby-lines`, `sl-you-wont-die`), "a victim that can't perish"
(`ucn-nightmare-freddy-cant-perish`). The monsters with the heaviest lines are FNaF4's, and FNaF4's guilty
party is the brother: "remade, but not by you", "this time, there is more than an illusion to fear", "we know
who our friends are" (`ucn-nightmare-freddy-toysnhk`, `ucn-nightmare-fredbear-lines`). "The one you should not
have killed" suits a death he caused and did not mean (`fnaf4-give-fredbear-a-kiss`) better than one victim
among a murderer's many. A whisper says "He tried to release you" of a man for whom "there was a way out
planned" (`ucn-orville-he-tried-to-release`, `ffps-completion-brave-volunteer`). The Puppet does not hate him
(`ucn-puppet-lines`). And the game's one spoken ending tells its player that the demon is someone else and that he may rest
(`ucn-omc-ending`), which carries the Cassette Man's sorting through: Hell for the father, rest for the son.

**Where that case fails.** "This time, death cannot save you" (`ucn-nightmarionne-lines`) has no sense for a
man death never saved. "Your wickedness", "your misdeeds" and the cartoon of a serial lurer (`ucn-nightmare-lines`,
`ucn-jack-o-chica-toysnhk`, `ucn-toy-chica-hsy-premise`) describe a career, not one prank. And it has to seat
the crying child behind Golden Freddy, which R8 denies. So William leads, narrowly.

**Problems.**

1. **The confidences contradict each other.** If William is in the office, the old man cannot be talking to
   the man in the office (unless "the demon" is someone other than William, which the ruling does not propose
   and its moral map could not accept). So (2) needs the first half of (4), that the listener is not the man
   in the office. The ruling rates (4) as a whole at low-medium, calls it "the weakest part of the ruling",
   and rates (2) at medium. The half that (2) depends on has no rating of its own. A conclusion cannot be
   firmer than a condition it requires. (2) also rests wholly on (1), the status ruling, itself
   medium. *Fix:* split (4) into (4a) "the old man's listener is not the man in the office" and (4b) "the
   listener is the accuser". Rate (2) no higher than (4a). Unless (4a) can be argued to medium, (2) is
   "low-medium to medium: the model's lean".

2. **A second listener is available and is not considered: the person playing.** In his only other speaking
   scene the old man addresses the player about the game itself: "you have gone too deep into the code"
   (`fw-old-man-consequences`). UCN reuses that screen, leaves the player nothing to do but sink, and closes
   the program (`ucn-omc-ending-drowning`, `fw-drowning-ending`). Scott called UCN his "capstone"
   (`scott-2018-interview-ucn-capstone`, transcript). Read this way, "Leave the demon to his demons" tells the
   player to stop and leave the man in the office to the roster. It supports William with no change of
   listener inside the fiction. Its cost: the line then says nothing about the accuser, so (4b), "one victim is
   offered rest and will not take it", and the thesis table's use of `ucn-omc-ending` under "Rest for the
   victims" lose their record. *Fix:* put both listeners under (4a) and let (2) stand on (4a), not (4b).

3. **The argument offered for (4) leaves out half the trigger and breaks W3.** "The ending is reached by a
   night with every tormentor switched off but him. In the fiction the one who sets the tormentors on is the
   accuser." The scene opens only when the player, in the office, catches the fish (`ucn-omc-ending`), and
   the same controls then sink into the lake. Treating the night-select screen as an act inside the story
   uses a mechanic as text, which W3 forbids. And the premise rests on `ucn-summoned-performers`, an `implied`
   record whose note says "Who 'demanded' or 'requested' is unstated". One record that does help (4b) was in
   the memo and was dropped: Pigpatch's proverb about sitting by the river until an enemy's body floats by
   (`ucn-pigpatch-proverbs`). *Fix:* drop the set-up argument or amend W3; cite Pigpatch.

4. **Orville's line is scored for one side only.** The memo gave it two rows: "No matter how many times they
   burn us", W "conditionally", light; and "He tried to release you", M. The ruling keeps the first as a plain
   W and drops the second.
   - "He tried to release you" fits the volunteer's planned way out better than the man told "don't keep the
     Devil waiting" (`ffps-completion-old-friend-hell`).
   - The line separates "you" from "us", so the burnings are the speaker's. "The one figure shown in two fires
     is the rabbit" does not follow, and the same ruling later uses "burn us" for the fifth child.
   - If "he" tried to release the listener and the accuser refuses, UCN is not "the night the Cassette Man
     promised him". It is what the accuser does against the Cassette Man's attempt.
   - *Fix:* score the record "="; replace "the night the Cassette Man promised him" with wording the record
     allows, such as "a night one victim keeps him in"; soften row 1 ("promising one man what UCN then shows").

5. **"Most of you" is misread, here and in R28.** `ffps-completion-peace-perhaps-more` is followed at once by
   `ffps-completion-old-friend-hell`: "For most of you, I believe there is peace... Although, for one of you,
   the darkest pit of Hell has opened". The exception to "most" is the old friend. R16 says the phrase "had
   already left room for an exception" for the fifth child, and R28 quotes "for most of you, I believe" as a
   hedge on the children's release. The hedge is "I believe" alone. *Fix:* remove the phrase from "Why the
   fifth child"; in R28 quote "I believe" only.
