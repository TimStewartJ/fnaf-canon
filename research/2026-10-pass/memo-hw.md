# Memo: slice `hw` (Help Wanted, Curse of Dreadbear, Special Delivery, Help Wanted 2)

Evidence file: `src/data/evidence/hw.json`, now 163 records (66 Help Wanted, 14 Curse of Dreadbear, 28 Special Delivery, 51 Help Wanted 2, 4 Scott/scottgames). 40 records added, 101 of the original 123 changed (95 in substance, 6 only in tags or time label). Validator and type check pass.

Words used in this memo, because the games do not supply names:

- **the rabbit figure**: Help Wanted's anomaly (person id `glitchtrap`). No game prints or speaks "Glitchtrap"; the games say "the anomaly", "this thing", "this virus", "he" (`hw-tape-girl-calls-it-the-anomaly`).
- **the tape speaker**: the tester on the sixteen tapes (person id `tape-girl`). The credits give only "QA" (`hw-credits-tape-girl-qa`). "Tape Girl" is a fan label.
- **the follower**: the unnamed woman on the hidden call and whoever "Ness" is. "Vanny" is spoken only from Security Breach on; in Help Wanted 2 it appears only in the cast credits (`hw2-credits-vanny`).
- **the technician**: Help Wanted 2's player.

Dates: nothing in these four games prints a present-day year. Special Delivery's email dates are the real-world days the emails reached players (section 3, item 2).

## 1. Threads

### 1.1 The thing in the boards (holds up the whole post-fire story)

What the games establish:

- **Where it came from.** Fazbear Entertainment, the client, sent a VR studio old "junk - circuit boards and things like that" and told it to scan them to avoid programming; usable code "seemed to take hold by itself", "things started changing", and then "he started appearing" (`hw-tape-05`, `hw-tape-05-client-sent-junk-to-scan`). The company's own guide says its teams used "vintage control-boards almost like plug-and-play, digitally recreating performances and personalities from the past" (`hw-handunit-vintage-control-boards`), plus scanned photographs and "original performance routines" (`hw-handunit-scanned-photos-original-routines`). By the company's account every character in the VR game is a recreated "personality from the past"; the anomaly came in by the same door.
- **Which machine the boards came from is never said.** Help Wanted says only "looked pretty old". Special Delivery's scanner, asked which board, answers "Whatever Dan brought me" (`sd-which-board-unknown`).
- **What it looks like.** A grinning, man-shaped figure in a golden-yellow rabbit costume with a purple star-speckled vest, purple bow tie and glowing purple eyes, translucent at first (`hw-rabbit-figure-appearance`). It is a costumed person, not an animatronic, and it is yellow, not purple.
- **What it does to people.** It watched the tester through a window "like it was actually looking in" (`hw-tape-02`). The first tester, Jeremy, had nightmares, stopped reacting, stood "like he's talking to someone" (`hw-tape-07`) and, the tapes imply, cut his own face with a paper guillotine while wearing the visor (`hw-jeremy-self-injury-implied`). It "wants to escape... through someone plugged into this game" (`hw-tape-16`).
- **How it lives in the files.** It attached itself to the tape speaker's logs (`hw-tape-11`), hid in their protected storage (`hw-tape-12`, `hw-tape-room-glitchtrap`), was broken up with them (`hw-tape-14`), and is reassembled as the player collects them (`hw-tape-15`, `hw-glitchtrap-approaches-with-tapes`).
- **Its voice is borrowed.** The only speech heard from it in the hub is the tape speaker's own greeting, pitched down (`hw-glitchtrap-uses-tape-girl-voice`).
- **Its three outcomes in Help Wanted.** Pizza Party: it beckons the player behind a curtain in a birthday room and the player ends up on stage as a performer (`hw-pizza-party-birthday-room`, `hw-pizza-party-ending`). Merge: the player ends up on stage as the rabbit figure (`hw-merge-ending`). Trapped: it withdraws behind a sealed door, hushing the player, and leaves a green plush (`hw-trapped-ending`, `hw-trapped-ending-plush`).
- **The company knows.** "Visual artifacts" not to be touched (`hw-handunit-beta-artifacts`); "an undesirable anomaly entering the game's code" as a "funny fun fact" and legal disclaimer (`hw-handunit-anomaly-fun-fact`); a waiver against "accidental digital consciousness transference, real world manifestations of digital characters" (`hw-waiver-consciousness-transference`); the first team's warnings called "wholly untrustworthy" (`hw-handunit-first-dev-team-pulled`).
- **It gets out a second time.** In Special Delivery a different contractor, storing whole animatronics (`sd-contractor-storing-animatronics`), drills their boards out (`sd-contractor-circuit-boards-drilled`), is locked out after scanning the last set, and finds it has released "a virus... spreading really fast" (`sd-circuit-board-scan-virus`), traced to a Fazbear board (`sd-virus-traced-to-fe-board`). Fazbear's lawyers stop all work "especially in reference to any vintage hardware" one day after the lockout and four days before the contractor understands what happened (`sd-fe-legal-halt-vintage-hardware`, `sd-fe-halts-all-contractors`).
- **It is still in company systems in the Pizzaplex era.** In Help Wanted 2 the same figure corrupts training screens (`hw2-glitchtrap-corrupts-backstage`), appears as hand puppets (`hw2-glitchtrap-hand-puppets`), and a rabbit-costumed arm comes out of a recharge station in the main ending (`hw2-faz-force-ending`). In the secret ending a tiny copy of it is crushed and the achievement reads "System threats found. Repair complete." (`hw2-pq4-vanny-crushes-glitchtrap`, `hw2-pq4-consequences-achievement`).

What stays open: which machine; whether it is the dead man, a copy, or an imitation (section 2, Q1); whether the Trapped ending contained anything; whether the Special Delivery virus is the same code or a second copy from another board.

Connections outside the slice: the decoded Princess Quest line matches Scraptrap's and UCN's "I always come back" (`ffps-scraptrap-always-come-back`, `ucn-afton-always-come-back`); Security Breach's burnt rabbit animatronic rises from a recharge station (`security-breach-freddy-i-am-not-me`, `security-breach-burntrap-appearance`); RUIN's lure speaks only in replayed recordings of Gregory (`ruin-mimic-reuses-gregory-lines`); Secret of the Mimic says M2 "began as a copy of my program" (`secret-of-the-mimic-mail-received-5-min-ago`) and shows its victim put on stage inside a suit (`secret-of-the-mimic-ending-clock-out`), as Pizza Party does.

### 1.2 The follower: plush, mask, the woman on the call, Ness

What the games establish, in release order:

1. Help Wanted's player is on "the Fazbear Entertainment Quality Assurance team" (`hw-waiver-qa-team`). Following Tape 16 leaves them with a green rabbit plush (`hw-trapped-ending-plush`).
2. By August 8, 2019, the day of the first Security Breach teaser, Scott's site carried a hidden exchange in its source: "Stay the course." "I will." "Focus on my voice." "I will." "Don't let anyone lead you astray." "I won't." "Have you selected one?" "I have." It stayed under every Security Breach teaser until at least February 2021 (`scottgames-sb-source-stay-the-course`).
3. Curse of Dreadbear's Corn Maze secret yields a grinning white rabbit mask (`codb-corn-maze-rabbit-mask`). Wearing it and picking up the plush plays a woman answering someone unheard: "Yes, I hear you... There's no miscommunication" (`codb-mask-plush-voice-opening`), "Yes, I have it. I made it myself... no one suspects anything... I won't let you down. It will be fun." (`codb-mask-plush-voice`). The voice plays in first person, when the player wears the mask.
4. Special Delivery: Luis Cabrera, who monitors staff internet use, writes six emails to a colleague, "Ness". Her flagged searches run from 'torture' (`sd-ness-blood-eagle-calendar`) to "how to induce compliance in human subjects", "how to induce self-compliance(?)" and the single word "help" (`sd-ness-compliance-searches`), then "How far can a human being be cut in half before losing consciousness" (`sd-ness-cut-in-half-search`) and three "lifelike, human male rubber masks" (`sd-ness-rubber-masks`). She then takes an interest in his IT job (`sd-ness-interested-in-it`), and is last seen with her face to the screen, "in another world", with voices audible (`sd-ness-voices-at-screen`).
5. Help Wanted 2: the same mask design is ordinary staff equipment. A poster shows an employee lifting it: "CLOCKING OUT? ALREADY? TAKE OFF YOUR MASK!" (`hw2-poster-take-off-your-mask`). In Princess Quest IV the technician trades this mask for the green plush (`hw2-pq4-vanny-mask-trade`), and a giant rabbit-masked silhouette takes the plush and crushes what comes out of it (`hw2-pq4-elevator-claw-machine`, `hw2-pq4-vanny-crushes-glitchtrap`).

What stays open: the woman is never named in Help Wanted or Curse of Dreadbear; "Ness" is never called Vanessa; what "it" is ("I made it myself"); what "selected one" refers to.

Connections: Vanessa's therapy tapes name a Luis and a monitored online history (`security-breach-cd-vanessa-encrypted-conversation`), fake fur bought for a secret costume and "He said he would always be watching" (`security-breach-cd-vanessa-costume-fur`), and a transfer "to a different location" (`security-breach-cd-vanessa-transfer`). Security Breach's Princess Quest III is set in Help Wanted's hub and Corn Maze and ends at the Trapped ending's door; finishing it frees Vanessa (`security-breach-ending-redemption`). Scott's teasers pair a guard "PROTECT" with a rabbit "OBEY" (`scottgames-site-sb-protect`, `scottgames-site-sb-obey`). The sb-mimic file has no record of what Princess Quest III shows (see section 8 and the final note to the lead).

### 1.3 The cover story: the earlier games exist inside the story

What the games establish:

- Fazbear's own framing: a bad reputation "over the last few decades"; "some stories... loosely based on actual events" (`hw-intro-loosely-based-on-actual-events`); most "total fabrications from the mind of a complete lunatic (lawsuits pending)" (`hw-intro-complete-lunatic`); the VR game recreates "completely fictitious scenarios (lies) that you've been fed over the last several years" (`hw-intro-vr-game-to-move-past-ghost-stories`). Two time spans: the reputation is decades old, the "lies" a few years old.
- The posters that go with it: the developer's photo (`hw-intro-indie-developer-poster`) and a Freddy Fazbear's storefront under "CLOSED / ALL BUSINESS SUSPENDED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE." and police tape (`hw-intro-closed-police-line-poster`).
- The VR game's menu files its recreations under the games' titles: "FNAF 1" (`hw-hub-menu-fnaf-1`).
- The tape speaker's correction: the staff were told the game was to undo "a rogue indie game developer" (`hw-tape-13-cover-story`), but a hard drive of emails showed "Fazbear Entertainment hired the game developer. Those indie games were designed to conceal and make light of what happened" (`hw-tape-13-fe-hired-developer`).
- The denials: "Nothing was ever proven in a court of law." (`hw-game-over-court-of-law`), "Animatronics can't kill you." (`hw-game-over-animatronics-cant-kill`), "no skeletons in our closet" (`hw-handunit-credits-no-skeletons`), the waiver that "isn't at all based on... injuries associated with testing" (`hw-intro-waiver-not-based-on-testing-injuries`), "absolutely no hidden intent or purpose" (`codb-handunit-no-hidden-intent`).
- Inside the company, the old machines are real. Special Delivery staff write of "the original" Mangle that "was purportedly able to move along the ceiling" and of "our rebuilds" (`sd-rebuilds-of-original-animatronics`); a customer email speaks of "the classic Mangle animatronic from the original restaurants" (`sd-mangle-original-restaurants`); customers meet an unlisted, foul-smelling "vintage Bonnie model" (`sd-vintage-bonnie-reports`).
- The Pizzaplex keeps selling the old scenes: "FNAF 1/2/3 themed" shooting galleries with FNaF2's cake-minigame children as targets (`hw2-fazerblast-fnaf-themed`), a "Sister Location" section (`hw2-sister-location-section`), "a simulated Pizza Place training booth" (`hw2-first-aid-simulated-pizza-place`).
- Scott's ruling: he is not part of the lore; "Only the existence of a game developer that supposedly made video games based on actual events that may or may not be a cover up by Fazbear Entertainment is canon" (`scott-not-part-of-lore`; also `scott-2019-not-canon-list`, `scott-2019-help-wanted-is-canon` in the Scott file).

What stays open: how faithful the in-universe games are to the events; whether the cover-up is fact (the tape speaker says so, Scott says "may or may not").

### 1.5 Princess Quest (four games across three releases)

- **I (Help Wanted mobile).** Unlocked from a glitching object (`hw-mobile-princess-quest-unlock`). The Princess, carrying a lantern, lights torches, crosses a graveyard of six stones (`hw-mobile-princess-quest-graveyard`), and behind a purple-locked door meets a purple rabbit-eared mass; a line of glyphs appears and the game ends (`hw-mobile-princess-quest-final-room`). Fans decode the glyphs as "I ALWAYS COME BACK" and the audio as "I always come back. Let me out." (`hw-mobile-princess-quest-ending`). My check of the glyph image: word lengths 1-6-4-4 and repeated glyphs where A and C repeat, so the decode is credible, but the game prints no key.
- **I to III (Security Breach).** Security Breach calls the first "that old mobile game" (`security-breach-msg-pq1-maint-log`) and has no record of the third cabinet (`security-breach-msg-out-of-order`). The third retraces Help Wanted and Curse of Dreadbear: hub, Corn Maze, mask and plush, the sealed door. Completion frees Vanessa (`security-breach-ending-redemption`).
- **IV (Help Wanted 2).** Unlocked by the six memories and a corrupted token (`hw2-pq4-unlock`). Marquee "Princess Quest IV", title screen "Princess Quest (Alpha)" (`hw2-pq4-alpha-take-it`). An old man gives the sword: "You are Won. And you remain." (`hw2-pq4-old-man`). The Princess reaches out ("Take it") and the player continues inside the game (`hw2-pq4-player-enters-game`). A graveyard pairs six stones with the six dolls (`hw2-pq4-graveyard-order`); a side chest holds a Bonnie mask captioned "This looks familiar..." (`hw2-pq4-bonnie-mask`). The old man trades the plush for the player's mask: "It is a fair trade" (`hw2-pq4-vanny-mask-trade`). Then Sister Location's elevator, a claw machine, the giant rabbit-masked figure, Moon carrying the plush, and the crush (`hw2-pq4-elevator-claw-machine`, `hw2-pq4-vanny-crushes-glitchtrap`). Achievement "Consequences": "System threats found. Repair complete." (`hw2-pq4-consequences-achievement`).

What stays open: who or what the Princess is; who the old man is; whether the fourth game's ending is an event or a branch.

Connections: the old man's sprite is Security Breach's; the achievement name "Consequences" is the one in-game link to FNaF World's and UCN's Old Man Consequences (`fw-old-man-consequences`, `ucn-omc-ending`). RUIN shows the cabinet in the hideout run through by the Princess's sword (`ruin-vanny-hideout-glitchtrap-shadow`).

### 1.6 The first studio: Jeremy and the tape speaker

The tapes are numbered in the order found; Tape 1 warns "they may not be in order" (`hw-tape-01`). By content the events run:

2. Jeremy has nightmares and declines; the tape speaker is groomed to replace him (`hw-tape-07`); management builds a paper trail because "he'd seen something" (`hw-tape-08`).
3. One early morning the supply room is "glowing" (`hw-tape-06`); it holds a guillotine paper slicer Jeremy knew about (`hw-tape-04`); something "like a Halloween mask" and "ink" lie on the floor, and Jeremy stands in the testing room in the visor, shirt front black (`hw-tape-09`). 6. She finds the hard drive of emails (`hw-tape-13-cover-story`, `hw-tape-13-fe-hired-developer`).
7. Last day: it has moved into her logs (`hw-tape-11`, `hw-tape-12`); she fragments them (`hw-tape-14`), fears they are a Trojan Horse (`hw-tape-15`), and leaves the kill instructions (`hw-tape-16`). Tape 1 is her covering note: the studio is "shutting down" (`hw-tape-01`).

### 1.10 Recreated scenes inside company software (weak as evidence of events)

Levels of Help Wanted and Help Wanted 2 are Fazbear products depicting earlier places. They show what the company chooses to stage, not what happened. ## 2. Contested questions

### Q1. What is the rabbit figure: the dead man himself, a copy of him on old hardware, or a machine imitating him?

**What the records say outright.** It is code that came off old control boards the company supplied (`hw-tape-05`, `hw-handunit-vintage-control-boards`). The company describes its method as "digitally recreating... personalities from the past". Nothing in Help Wanted, Curse of Dreadbear or Special Delivery names it, names the machine, or mentions Afton.

**For "a copy" (Afton-derived code).**
- The stated mechanism is copying: a personality reproduced from a board (`hw-handunit-vintage-control-boards`).
- Special Delivery's mechanics say the same in general: a CPU carries "a whole new personality" and can be put in any body (`sd-cpu-whole-new-personality`), and rebuilt machines speak the originals' lines, including claims of identity: Baby's "something bad... inside of me" (`sd-circus-baby-something-bad-inside`), Springtrap's "I'm...back" (`sd-springtrap-did-not-save-the-others`). A copy says what the original said. That removes "I always come back" (`hw-mobile-princess-quest-ending`) as proof of the man's survival: a copy would say it too.
- Its form is the killer's working disguise, a person in a yellow rabbit costume, not the wrecked animatronic (`hw-rabbit-figure-appearance`): a self-image, or a stored performance, rather than a body.
- Its staging is his: a lure with cake and a birthday room, a child-sized victim ending on stage inside a performer (`hw-pizza-party-favorite-cake-pizza`, `hw-pizza-party-birthday-room`, `hw-pizza-party-ending`); Springtrap watches from the FNaF3 office on the way (`hw-pizza-party-springtrap-window`).

**For "the man himself".** Only the decoded line and the series habit of his returning (`ffps-scraptrap-always-come-back`, `ucn-afton-always-come-back`). No record in this slice shows memory of his family, his name, or his death. Special Delivery's "vintage Bonnie" with a smell (`sd-vintage-bonnie-reports`) is evidence that a body persists somewhere, not that the code is him.

**For "an imitation" (the learning endoskeleton of Secret of the Mimic and RUIN).** There is in-game support, and it does not depend on the books, but all of it is indirect and most of it arrives after RUIN:
- The figure speaks in a replayed human recording (`hw-glitchtrap-uses-tape-girl-voice`); RUIN's lure does exactly that with Gregory's lines (`ruin-mimic-reuses-gregory-lines`).
- The same costumed arm that holds out the six "memories" holds out the White Tiger doll on code 1979 (`hw2-memory-dolls`, `hw2-mimic-teaser-1979-white-tiger`), and Mystic Hippo calls that "the distant past" (`hw2-mystic-hippo-gift-distant-past`). The White Tiger belongs to the Murray story (`secret-of-the-mimic-ending-story-time`). Whatever hands these out holds mementos of both stories.
- Help Wanted 2's main ending puts the rabbit arm in a recharge station and cuts to RUIN's opening (`hw2-faz-force-ending`); RUIN's villain is the endoskeleton (`ruin-mimic-reveal`).
- Secret of the Mimic states that the Mimic's mind is a program that has already been copied between bodies (`secret-of-the-mimic-mail-received-5-min-ago`), that "WA" took over Murray's catalogue (`secret-of-the-mimic-mail-confidential-wa`), and that it puts its victims on stage in suits (`secret-of-the-mimic-ending-clock-out`, `secret-of-the-mimic-previous-technicians`), which is Pizza Party's ending.
- Cut lines separate an "it" that is using the player from a "his" whose code has nearly gone (`hw2-unused-vanny-dont-help-it-escape`); cut content, so weak.

**Against the imitation reading.** The memories it keeps are the six (`hw2-memory-dolls`, `hw2-pq4-graveyard-order`); the Freddy memory is won from a Springtrap cutout in a fire, with a scream reportedly shared with Princess Quest III (`hw2-fazerblast-fire-springtrap`, `hw2-springtrap-cutout-pq3-scream`); RUIN and Secret of the Mimic place the endoskeleton as a body sealed underground "for a really long time" (`ruin-gregory-mxes-explanation`), not as code in a VR studio.

**Verdict.** For Help Wanted, Curse of Dreadbear and Special Delivery the records favour **a copy on old hardware**: medium. "The man himself" is not established: low. "The Mimic" as the Help Wanted anomaly is not established: low; but "what reaches RUIN wears the rabbit's face and is the Mimic" has real in-game support: low to medium. The records allow, and I think suggest, **two things rather than one**: board-borne rabbit code that is finally removed in Princess Quest III and IV, and the endoskeleton under the Pizzaplex that outlasts it. The current theory's label "Afton-derived code... an imitation, a copy, or the man himself all remain possible" is supported. It should stop calling the figure a purple rabbit, and it should cite the voice, the White Tiger, the recharge station and the CPU records, which are the actual evidence on each side.

### Q14. How does "the games are an in-universe cover story" fit a model that treats the earlier games' events as real?

So the cover story does not unmake the earlier games' events: high. What it changes: (a) inside the story the public has a reason to disbelieve, which is how the brand survives; (b) the in-universe games may distort, and the records never say where; (c) anything shown inside Help Wanted's or Help Wanted 2's levels is a company depiction and should not be cited as an observation of events (`hw-pns-freddy-child-items` is used that way in the "murders" chapter and in `relations.ts`). ## 4. What the theory leaves unconnected

Proposed treatments, in story order. Confidence is for the reading, not the records.

1. **The company scans its own past (new beat after `s-fire`).** After the fire Fazbear Entertainment reissues its history as entertainment: indie games it commissioned to "make light of what happened", then a VR game built from "vintage control-boards" (`hw-tape-13-fe-hired-developer`, `hw-handunit-vintage-control-boards`). The board scan brings a "personality from the past" into the code (`hw-tape-05`). Belongs at the head of the "afterimage" part. High for the sequence, medium for what the personality is.

2. **Jeremy and the tape speaker: the cost and the witness.** The theory uses the tapes only as a source for the boards. The human story is what shows how the thing works: it singles out whoever is in the headset, gets into their sleep, has them hurt themselves, and the company documents them as unreliable (`hw-tape-07`, `hw-tape-08`, `hw-jeremy-self-injury-implied`, `hw-intro-waiver-not-based-on-testing-injuries`). The witness then builds a trap that the player springs (`hw-tape-16`, `hw-trapped-ending`). Belongs in "digital". Medium to high.

3. **"Someone plugged in": the vessel as a repeating position.** `hw-tape-16` states the aim. The records then give five people in that position: Jeremy (visor), the Help Wanted player (headset), Ness (screen, `sd-ness-voices-at-screen`), the technician (mask, `hw2-remove-mask-real-world`), Cassie (mask, `ruin-mask-bot-helpi-vanni`). 4. **The plush-and-mask chain and the page-source dialogue.** See problem 9. This is the spine that makes Help Wanted, Curse of Dreadbear, Special Delivery, Security Breach and Help Wanted 2 one story about one follower. Belongs in "vanny". Medium to high.

5. **Ness's emails read as a sequence.** Flagged searches; "how to induce self-compliance(?)" and "help"; cutting a person in half; rubber masks of men's faces; then she starts cultivating the man who monitors her; then the trance (`sd-ness-compliance-searches`, `sd-ness-cut-in-half-search`, `sd-ness-rubber-masks`, `sd-ness-interested-in-it`, `sd-ness-voices-at-screen`). 6. **The second outbreak and the company's reflex.** Legal stops "vintage hardware" work before the contractor knows it has a virus (`sd-fe-legal-halt-vintage-hardware`), then stops answering (`sd-virus-traced-to-fe-board`); in Help Wanted the client clears the drawers overnight (`hw-tape-05-client-sent-junk-to-scan`). Belongs in "digital" as the argument for "copy", and as a bridge to "mimic". Medium.

8. **The cover story gets its own section.** Currently one clause in "digital" and one line on the home page. It needs the three layers (events, in-universe games, VR game), the company's internal admissions in Special Delivery, the Pizzaplex still selling "FNAF 1/2/3" (`hw2-fazerblast-fnaf-themed`), and the rule that company recreations are not observations (Q14).
