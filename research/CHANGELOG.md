# Model changelog

## 2026-10-02: the theory pass (rulings R1 to R33)
The model was argued again from the evidence up, and the theory was rewritten to the result. R-numbers below are the rulings of this pass.
- **How the pass was run:**
  - Six audits of the evidence base, one per slice: FNaF1 to FNaF3; FNaF4, FNaF World and Sister Location; Pizzeria Simulator and Ultimate Custom Night; Help Wanted, Curse of Dreadbear, Special Delivery and Help Wanted 2; Security Breach, RUIN, Into the Pit and Secret of the Mimic; Scott's statements and teasers. Each analyst checked the records against transcripts and screenshots, added what was missing and wrote a memo of the contested questions.
  - An adjudicator ruled on every contested question, with a confidence and the wording each ruling requires.
  - Three adversarial reviews of the rulings. Nothing was overturned. Many confidences were lowered, several statements were split into what is shown and what is inferred, and one ruling was added (R33, on what persists of Afton after the fire).
  - The theory was rewritten as 35 chapters in 10 parts, with a storyline of 20 beats. Every record is now cited by a chapter, filed under one as further evidence, or listed as outside the story with a reason.
  - The other layers were then brought in line: people, relations, family trees, places, the timeline, dates and deaths; the home page; The Box, Theorists & Scott, and Scott & Faith.
  - Everything written was then checked, sentence by sentence, against the records and the rulings. Six fact-check reports covered the chapters, the storyline and the list of what is outside the story. Four covered the other layers: people; relations and family trees; the timeline, dates, deaths and places; the home page, the three essays and the interface's fixed text. Every writer then made one revision pass on its report.
  - The rulings' wording was corrected after those checks. Round four made 46 corrections and round 4b made 22; neither changed a grade or a conclusion. One change is to shape. The order of events now tells the 1983 week, then the child outside, then the five, as the storyline and the timeline do; both placings are conventions.
  - A records editor then went over the titles, claims and notes of every record, so that each says only what is on the screen or the tape. Titles use descriptors, and what a record takes from the wiki's report is said to be reported.
  - Two readers read all 35 chapters in order. A first reader, who had seen none of the planning, wrote a memo on how the text reads. The lead author wrote a memo on consistency: each shared fact told the same way in every chapter. Every writer then made a final pass and applied both.
  - One record was added: Security Breach's cast credits, which give "Vanessa" and "Vanny" to two performers as two parts (from a fan wiki's transcription of the credits). A credit names a voiced part and not a person, so the list does not say two women, but it counts against one woman. Round 4c of the rulings' wording followed and changed one grade: that Vanny is Vanessa went from medium-high to medium (R19).
  - The records editor's second pass went over dates and tags. After it no record carries a year after 1987, and every approximate date states its kind: derived, estimated, or taken from a teaser. The other layers were then read once more against the records as they stood.
  - Last, the text was verified independently against the records as they then stood. Four verifiers read every sentence that the final pass had changed in the chapters and the storyline, and each chapter once from the top; the owners of the other layers read their layers the same way. The verifiers reported 45 findings, 6 of them statements that the records or the rulings did not carry (a van that is not white, a year denied outright, a guide called a recording), and all were applied; the layer owners changed about 200 entries. A last round of the rulings' wording (4d) then brought the rulings level with the records and the text.
  - A second record was added in that step: Pizzeria Simulator's catalogue lists Rockstar Bonnie as an animatronic the franchisee can buy, which a sentence in the chapter on Ultimate Custom Night had said without a record.
  - A second reader then read the finished text in order, as a newcomer would, and judged it "mostly" compelling, readable and enjoyable. It named three obstacles: things told a second time, paragraphs under "What pushes back" that had grown long, and places where a reader who has not played the newer games loses his footing. Its 26 cuts, moves and headings were applied, and every writer then rewrote the passages that a cut could not mend. "The model" went from 261 uses to 227, and the mean length of a "What pushes back" paragraph from 164 words to 125.

## 2026-09-28: Scott & Faith
- **Research:** Scott's Christian background from primary sources. The key source is the Geeks Under Grace written interview (2014-10-25), plus the 2021 Reddit post and archived Hope Animation pages. Hope Animation was David Hutter's UK Christian media site that hosted Scott's films; it was **not** Scott's studio, contrary to a common assumption.
- **New evidence (Scott's words):** twelve years of Christian films and games; 'No' to hidden Christian messages (answering about FNaF1 and vice symbolism); faith and work; favourite verse Psalm 46:10 ('Be still'); the beaver-game origin; 'I can't explain Golden Freddy'; The Desolate Hope; 'I'm a Christian… I believe in God' (2021). Game: ffps-completion-peace-perhaps-more.
- **New theory section 'faith' (medium):** the games' own afterlife vocabulary is read literally, not as allegory. Rest lines go to victims; the only Hell line goes to the killer; Remnant holds souls away from rest. The map is labelled as a reading, since the games never show final destinations.
- **What changed in the model:** no identifications. UCN gained a supporting sentence (FFPS names Hell only for the killer; UCN's secret ending separates the player from 'the demon'), still medium. Golden Freddy notes that Scott disclosed no explanation in 2014.
- **Ruled out:** the vice allegory for FNaF1's animatronics (Scott, 2014). Noted but not used: the Cassette Man's 'be still' echoing Psalm 46:10.
- **New page /author**, a community interaction (i-gug), a home Q&A and a Method paragraph on author context.
- **A reviewer pass corrected:** 'promises peace' became 'believes'; the moral map was downgraded from supported to consistent; the scope of the 2014 'No' was narrowed; the Desolate Hope-to-FNaF bridge was removed; 'first afterlife language' was fixed (FNaF World's 'Rest.' is earlier).

## 2026-09-26: the plush's pupils
