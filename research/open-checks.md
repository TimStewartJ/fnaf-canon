# Open checks: records to add, and things nobody could verify from here

Written at the end of the October 2026 theory pass. The archive's conclusions rest on its records, and a record
is only as good as how it was read. This file lists what the pass could not check, and the records that would
let a hedge in the theory be dropped or would test a ruling directly. R-numbers are the rulings in
`rulings.md`. When one of these is done, add or correct the record, then look at the chapter's "what would
change this".

## How the records were read

- **Game dialogue, tapes and on-screen text** come from fan-wiki transcripts, except where a record says it
  was read on screen in a recording of play: about thirty records of FNaF4, FNaF World and Sister Location,
  and those of Security Breach, RUIN, Secret of the Mimic and Into the Pit that were read on 3 October 2026
  from one player's recordings kept at archive.org. They are cited without a tag, like any quoted line.
- **Images** on file (170) carry captions written from the image; the last verification looked again at only
  some of them. Where a record describes a screen that is not on file, it says "(as described)", or "(read
  from an image)" when the wiki's image was looked at and not kept.
- **Scenes described from a fan wiki's summary and not watched** carry "(as described)" or "(a fan wiki's
  account)" wherever a chapter leans on them.
- **Scott's posts** were verified at their source or in an archive of it. His two video interviews (2018 and
  2024) are held as machine transcripts and were not checked by ear: "(a transcript)".
- **The cast lists** of Sister Location, Pizzeria Simulator, Help Wanted and Help Wanted 2 are a fan wiki's
  transcription of the credits. Security Breach's was read from a recording of its credits roll and matches
  the transcription line for line (`security-breach-credits-cast-list`).
- Nothing was checked in play. Nobody in this pass ran a game. A recording of someone else's play is the
  nearest the pass came, and a record read from one says which recording and where in it.

## Checks that need the game, a capture or a recording

On 3 October 2026 this list was worked through against recordings of one player's play kept at archive.org
(the picture only; the sound was not heard). What a recording settled is under "Settled" below. What it did not
is still open, and each entry says what was looked through.

**Still open**

1. **Secret of the Mimic: the work order and the credential, seen in play.** The year 1979 ends the official
   teaser; in the game it was read from images of a work order (9/07/79) and a credential whose placement is
   unconfirmed (R2, R24). Every date for that night is marked as the teaser's. Looked for in a recording
   (Part 1, 18:15 to 20:06): forms with a Fazbear letterhead are in the van, but no date can be read and no
   credential was made out. It needs the game, or a capture that stops on the papers.
2. **Secret of the Mimic: that the cabinet F10-N4 is the first machine's mind.** The model joins it from four
   records. A line of the game that states it would make R21 (1) "shown". Twelve records of M1 and of the voice
   speaking as Fiona carry the person tag `f10-n4` on the strength of that join. The subtitles call the
   cabinet's voice "F10 N4" and then "FIONA", never M1 (`secret-of-the-mimic-reveal-subtitle-labels`). One join
   now has both its ends in the game's words: the cabinet says "I have a message for him" and sends the
   technician to the house (`secret-of-the-mimic-cabinet-message-for-edwin`), and the unsigned message begins
   "Edwin, I am sending you a technician" and goes on "a copy of my program"
   (`secret-of-the-mimic-mail-received-5-min-ago`). It is still a join.
3. **Secret of the Mimic: where on the radio the cabinet's voice first takes over.** Three records say it is not
   stated, and the subtitles do not mark it: in the stretches read from a recording, every line of the radio
   voice is labelled "DISPATCH:" until the cabinet, where the label becomes "F10 N4:" and then "FIONA:". The
   hours between the first briefing and the cabinet were not read. By a fan wiki's listing the sound files are
   named "DISPATCH" throughout, but for twelve named "Dispatch F10N4" just before the cabinet.
4. **Secret of the Mimic: the bodies in character suits.** The voice's line is read on screen (Part 1, 1:24:10
   to 1:24:26, labelled "DISPATCH:") and is as the record has it
   (`secret-of-the-mimic-previous-technicians`). The bodies are still not seen: costumed figures lie near the
   cabinet's room (Part 7, 44:58 to 45:15), and nothing on screen says they are bodies. The text now gives
   them as a fan wiki's account.
5. **Secret of the Mimic: Fiona's and David's deaths, and the Murrays' marriage.** No subtitle states either
   death, and none says "wife": an index of the subtitles of one whole playthrough has no such line. One
   staff message does say "her death" of Fiona, "Did they fake her death for insurance money?", read on
   screen (Part 6, 27:56; `secret-of-the-mimic-mail-heard-fiona`). It is a staff member's word and does not
   say how she died. Nothing is found for David.
6. **Secret of the Mimic: the two yellow prototypes.** Their names come from file labels
   (`secret-of-the-mimic-rnd-proto-pizzeria`). How they look in play would let the floor of the five's bracket
   (1980) rest on something seen. One prototype was seen in a recording (Part 7, 43:28: a dark metal bear
   with a top hat on a small stage); the yellow two were not found.
7. **RUIN: the costumes in the sealed room.** That they are Murray's is the wiki's comparison
   (`ruin-mimic-lair-mcm-mascot-suits`). The room is too dark in the recording looked at (RUIN Part 7, 5:36 to
   6:10) to make out any costume.
8. **RUIN: the rabbit's shadow in the hideout** (`ruin-vanny-hideout-glitchtrap-shadow`): still described from
   the wiki; not found in a recording.
9. **Ultimate Custom Night: the second voice.** A whispering voice is reported under three rare lines
   (`ucn-happy-frog-never-rest`, `ucn-nedd-bear-forever`, `ucn-orville-he-tried-to-release`). Whether it is
   one voice across the three, and whether any other line has it, needs a listening check. "He runs the
   night" (R16 (3)) would be firmer or weaker for it.
10. **Help Wanted: the clip that plays near the rabbit in the hub**, by ear, against the first tape's greeting
    (`hw-glitchtrap-uses-tape-girl-voice`).
11. **FNaF2: the fifth body in Give Gifts, Give Life.** One frame, reported by the wiki
    (`fnaf2-give-gifts-give-life`). It is one of the four counts in which the yellow bear marks the one more.
    No recording of the minigame was found (one long replay of the game and the 2014 series were looked
    through).
12. **Three fan videos nobody in the pass watched.** Game Theory's "Why FNAF Will Never End" (2015) was the
    source of a guess on the Box page and is now attached to nothing. Whether "The FINAL Theory", part 2,
    argued that Mike Schmidt is Michael decides that Theorists entry's source and one count on the entry for
    that video. The date of "FNAF 6, What was in the BOX?" (given as 2018-02-04) was not checked.
13. **The scenes still told second-hand that were not reached.** Sister Location's credits roll (the tag
    "(a fan wiki's account)" stays on the comparison of its cast list with Pizzeria Simulator's), Glamrock
    Freddy's "What if I am not the first Glamrock Freddy?" on screen, and eleven scenes behind the tags
    "(as described)" and "(a fan wiki's account)": the alley ending of Security Breach, the comics in RUIN,
    the earthquake notice, Help Wanted 2's gift-pushing bear and its fourth Princess Quest, FNaF2's yellow
    bear in the office, FNaF1's clipping on the mascot costume, Sister Location's keypad, the Princess Quest
    graveyard on the mobile port, Into the Pit's secret minigames, and FNaF4's eighth night.

  still the wiki's.

- **Help Wanted 2's main ending** (`hw2-faz-force-ending`): watched (Part 14, 26:54 to 28:48). The girl is
  shown full face and no name is printed; what the robot holds out is a white rabbit mask like RUIN's
  Security Mask. Only a hand shows at the recharge station's door: "rabbit-costumed" is the wiki's. Its place
  between Security Breach and RUIN is still "most likely" (R22).
- **Princess Quest III's ending** (`security-breach-pq3-help-wanted-hub-trapped-door`; Security Breach Part 9,
  3:22:40 to 3:25:11): "This door appears to be locked.", a key in place of the mask and plush, and "ERROR"
  on the cabinet in the comic that follows. The copy of Help Wanted's hub, the door like that game's and the
  scream are still the wiki's.
- **The Burn It All Down ending** (Part 10, 1:02:32): the place burns and comes down from inside as the two
  run out. The building is not shown from outside or afterwards, so R19 (4) gets "it was coming down when
  they left" and no more. No ceiling and no burner rooms are shown where the mass takes the rabbit.
- **Security Breach's credits roll** (`security-breach-credits-cast-list`): one performer to a line,
  "Vanessa" and "Vanny" on two lines, as transcribed.
- **Pizzeria Simulator's last scene** (`ffps-completion-fire`; Part 5, 9:43 to 10:12): Scrap Baby, Molten
  Freddy and the salvaged rabbit among flames, one after another; Lefty is in none of the shots. Its credits
  roll prints each part above its performer (`ffps-credits-william-afton`).
- **Special Delivery's inbox** (`sd-mail-server-advisory`; Part 1, 10:51; Part 2, 2:40): each row carries a
  time of day (as "11:31:31 PM") and no date. Every row seen was from the session being played, so whether
  an older email is listed with a date is not known. R24 (2) stands.

## Left as they are, on purpose

- **Record ids are stable handles.** No id was renamed or deleted in the pass, so a few say more than their
  records now do (`security-breach-rooftop-vanessa-alive`, `secret-of-the-mimic-log-m1-as-fiona`,
  `hw-glitchtrap-uses-tape-girl-voice`) or carry a fan label (`…-toysnhk`, `fnaf2-shadow-freddy`,
  `into-the-pit-prologue-mci`). Record pages print them labelled "Record id"; they are not part of the claim.
- **People tags were not swept as a whole.** The rule (a tag stays only where the game at that spot names the
  person or shows the figure) was applied to the records the fields pass named and the ones it passed through.
- **Teaser records.** Twenty-seven records of a teaser picture with printed words are graded "shown" and
  eleven "stated"; either can be defended, and they were not made uniform.
- **"The newest game".** The storyline's first beat calls Secret of the Mimic the newest game, which is true of
  the archive's list of releases today. It needs changing when a later game is added to `games.json`.
- **Safari was not tested.** WebKit could not be started on the machine that built the site; the interface
  checks ran in Chromium.

## Decisions that are the owner's

- **"Cassidy".** Scott used the name in 2020, in posts about a licensed product and two rejected film scripts
  (three records). The archive still does not use it for any child, because no game prints it.
- **The Survival Logbook** stays out, as decided on 1 October 2026.
- **Mike Schmidt.** Three chapters say that the strongest evidence usually cited for "Mike Schmidt is Michael"
  is in a book the archive does not admit, without saying what the book says (R13, R27). If that sentence is
  unwelcome, it can go from `michael`, `open` and `fnaf1-restaurant` with no other change.
- **The Theorists entry "The Cassette Man is Henry"** is kept, with text that says the name comes from the
  novels and is not used here. It is the one place a book-only name is printed.
- **Recordings of play as a way of checking.** Forty-odd records now say they were read on screen in a
  recording of someone else's play (one player's, kept at archive.org), and ten images are frames cut from
  those recordings, with the player's camera cropped out. The words on the screen are the game's, so this
  stays inside the source rule; if recordings should not be used, the records say which they are
  (`verifiedVia`), and the ten images are the ones whose credit points at archive.org.
- **Search and the names the archive does not use.** A search for "Henry", "Charlie" or "Emily" finds nobody
  and says that the archive uses only names the games print or speak. Making such a name lead to the
  Cassette Man's page is one alias away, and was left undone on purpose.
